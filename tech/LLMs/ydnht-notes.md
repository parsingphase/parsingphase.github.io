
All of this, of course, ignores the externalities - the ethical environmental, social and health costs. Unfortunately, some consider these optional.

Coding isn’t more appropriate to LLMs just because it’s “familiar” to the machine, or because “computers understand software”. Computers don’t run code in the languages humans usually write it in; everything has to be converted, either ahead of time or on the fly, into binary code. What’s executed does not naturally translate back into “source code”, any more than seeing a smashed vase would enable you to create a new one.

Agents are neither "self-improving" nor "going rogue".

Loss of usability of models may not depend on model collapse; availability and cost also depends on the behavior of companies currently offering addiction pricing models, even outages. Possible future political constraints, too.

Sources of code for LLM training have mixed quality and may not be intended for production use (eg, examples that take shortcuts, embed tokens for simplicity)

Developer quality as relevant to mentoring.

Consideration of just how much software controls in the modern world - ie everything, but particularly critical systems like utilities, air traffic control, communication - even food distribution. 

Developer burnout as a further cost/risk to a robust community.
